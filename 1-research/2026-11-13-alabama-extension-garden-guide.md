# Source note: A Guide to Starting a Community Garden (Alabama Cooperative Extension System)

- **Link:** https://www.aces.edu/blog/topics/business-community-urban/a-guide-to-starting-a-community-garden/
- **Published by:** Alabama Cooperative Extension System (by Rudy Pacumbaba), 2023-06-29
- **Read:** 2026-11-13

This note is a summary in our own words. It is never edited after it's added; corrections go on the wiki pages.

## What it says that matters to the garden

- **People first:** about five interested families (or a similar group) is the minimum to start and keep a garden going. Form a committee early, with at least a chair, a secretary and a treasurer, meet monthly, and involve local officials who can connect the group with city or county help.
- **Land:** look for a lot with six to eight hours of sun a day, fairly flat and free of large rubble, ideally fenced with a gate wide enough for a vehicle, within walking distance or a short drive. Start with three possible sites, which makes success more likely.
- **The owner:** using land without permission is illegal. The county tax assessor's office can identify an unknown owner. Groups often lease a site for $1 a year; ask for at least three years. Offer a "hold harmless" waiver, and be ready to buy liability insurance.
- **Water:** ask the local water provider whether the site has a water meter and to assess it. A site that had water service before costs less to connect. A garden needs a meter of about half an inch to one inch, depending on size, and a new water line can be a large cost.
- **Soil:** test it for fertility, pH and heavy metals, through the local county Extension office.
- **Cost:** a garden with just the basics (the guide's examples include fencing and water) typically costs $2,500 to $5,000, so groups often build in phases.
