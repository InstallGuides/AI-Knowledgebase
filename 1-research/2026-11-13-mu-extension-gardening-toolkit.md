# Source note: Community Gardening Toolkit (University of Missouri Extension)

- **Link:** https://extension.missouri.edu/publications/mp906
- **Published by:** University of Missouri Extension (publication MP906, by Bill McKelvey). First published October 2017; the page lists a March 2026 revision.
- **Read:** 2026-11-13

This note is a summary in our own words. It is never edited after it's added; corrections go on the wiki pages.

## What it says that matters to the garden

- **Start with a meeting** of everyone interested, with someone taking notes. The meeting should settle what kind of garden it is (separate plots, one shared garden, or both), who it serves, whether land is available, whether gardeners pay a fee (possibly on a sliding scale), how much time people can give, and who will join a leadership team.
- **Evaluating a site:** at least six hours of direct sun a day for fruit and vegetables; access to water; enough room; fairly flat; close to the gardeners; visible (safer, and draws neighbors' support); whether a truck can get in; and how the land was used before, because old city lots can have poor or contaminated soil. Test the soil, for nutrients and heavy metals, before agreeing to anything with a landowner.
- **Finding the owner:** with the site's address, the county tax assessor's office or website can usually tell you who owns it.
- **Resources to line up:** tools, compost and mulch, seeds, a tool shed, fencing, mowing, liability insurance, and local help such as Extension Master Gardeners or businesses willing to donate.
- **Gardener guidelines** should cover fees, plot upkeep, shared work, what may be planted, pesticides, water use (for example, whether it can be left running), and what happens when a rule is broken.
- **Common challenges:** community gardens take a lot of management and upkeep, and gardeners and leaders come and go from year to year.
