# Question: when is our last-frost date?

**Last updated:** 2026-11-13
**Review by:** 2027-11-13
**Status:** open

The group wants to plant by spring, and its notes say to check the average last-frost date before picking a planting week ([mission](../../0-project-management/mission.md)).

## What we know

- **A hardiness zone isn't a frost date.** The USDA zone map is based on the average coldest winter temperature, and tells you which perennials survive the winter. It doesn't say when the last spring frost happens ([USDA](../../1-research/2026-11-13-usda-hardiness-zone-map.md)).
- **NOAA publishes the numbers we need.** Its climate normals cover almost 15,000 U.S. weather stations, and include the odds of frost and freeze dates and the length of the growing season ([NOAA](../../1-research/2026-11-13-noaa-climate-normals.md)). The current set covers 1991 to 2020.

## How to find out

1. Find the NOAA weather station nearest the garden ([NOAA](../../1-research/2026-11-13-noaa-climate-normals.md)).
2. Look up its last spring frost date, and how likely a frost is after that date.
3. Write the date and its source on this page, then move the answer to a topic page on planting.
