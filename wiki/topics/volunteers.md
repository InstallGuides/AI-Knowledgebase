# Volunteers

**Last updated:** 2026-11-13

## What the group has planned

Find about five volunteers, make a flyer, start an email list, and ask the library about a sign-up table on a Saturday ([mission](../../0-project-management/mission.md)).

## How many people it takes

About **five interested families**, or a group of similar size, is the minimum to start a garden and keep it going ([Alabama Extension](../../1-research/2026-11-13-alabama-extension-garden-guide.md)). That matches the group's target.

## Organizing them

- **Hold a first meeting** of everyone interested, with someone taking notes, to settle what kind of garden it is, who it serves, and who will lead ([MU Extension](../../1-research/2026-11-13-mu-extension-gardening-toolkit.md)).
- **Form a committee early,** with at least a chair, a secretary and a treasurer, and meet monthly ([Alabama Extension](../../1-research/2026-11-13-alabama-extension-garden-guide.md)).
- **Plan regular workdays.** Gardens need a lot of upkeep, and people come and go from year to year ([MU Extension](../../1-research/2026-11-13-mu-extension-gardening-toolkit.md)).
- **Invite local officials,** who can connect the group with city or county help ([Alabama Extension](../../1-research/2026-11-13-alabama-extension-garden-guide.md)).
